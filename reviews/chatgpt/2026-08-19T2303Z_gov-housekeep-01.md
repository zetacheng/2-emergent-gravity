## 1. Governance purpose — PASS

The specification correctly addresses a methodological weakness exposed by recent verification work: an `INCONCLUSIVE` result can be methodologically correct while still containing a finite, explicit path toward resolution.

The amendment improves follow-up discipline without weakening the evidentiary standard required for a positive scientific verdict.

## 2. `INCONCLUSIVE` remains unchanged — PASS

The specification correctly preserves the original verdict.

`INCONCLUSIVE` remains `INCONCLUSIVE`.

The new mechanism affects only the disposition and follow-up classification of the result.

It does not authorize the executor or reviewer to promote a result merely because a plausible missing bridge has been identified.

## 3. Constructive-gap subclass — PASS

The new subclass:

`INCONCLUSIVE — CONSTRUCTIVE GAP IDENTIFIED`

is appropriately restricted to cases where:

- a candidate positive argument already exists;
- the missing steps are finite and explicit;
- the missing bridges can be investigated separately;
- completing those bridges could materially change the verdict.

This is a sufficiently high threshold to avoid turning every open question into a near-success.

## 4. Evidence-insufficient subclass — PASS

The complementary subclass:

`INCONCLUSIVE — EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT`

is correctly reserved for cases where no finite, explicit resolution path is presently identified.

This creates a meaningful distinction between:

`we do not yet know`

and

`we know exactly what remains to be established`.

## 5. Resolution-path symmetry — PASS

The specification improves on a one-sided `positive-resolution path` by requiring a symmetric:

`Resolution path`.

A valid path must state both:

- what outcome would support or upgrade the candidate argument; and
- what outcome would defeat or negatively resolve it.

A path that describes only the confirming outcome is explicitly incomplete.

This prevents the new mechanism from becoming institutionally biased toward positive results.

## 6. Executor boundary — PASS

The executor remains prohibited from supplying a missing scientific bridge merely because it appears obvious or likely.

The executor may identify and bound the missing bridge.

A separate reviewed derivation, measurement, or construction task is required to establish it.

This preserves the separation between verification and theory construction.

## 7. Reviewer/Researcher follow-up — PASS

The specification correctly shifts the methodological improvement to the follow-up layer:

`verification -> INCONCLUSIVE -> constructive-gap extraction -> bounded follow-up task -> re-verification`.

This avoids abandoning promising lines while maintaining strict evidentiary discipline.

## 8. PROJ-01 treatment — PASS

The specification correctly identifies PROJ-01 as the motivating example without promoting it to a positive result.

The existing verdict remains:

`INCONCLUSIVE`.

The candidate resolution path contains at least two distinct bridges:

1. the relationship between the Barnes-Rivers non-TT sector and the external complement discarded by the axis-TT construction;
2. the relationship between the IR improved effective kernel and the finite-spacing unimproved lattice diagnostic.

Both must be treated as separately investigable and potentially falsifiable.

## 9. No premature `FOUND` outcome — PASS

The specification correctly recognises that even establishing the Barnes-Rivers / axis-TT mapping may not, by itself, upgrade PROJ-01 to `FOUND`.

The finite-spacing-to-IR bridge may remain independently unresolved.

This prevents a partial bridge from being mistaken for full closure.

## 10. Retrospective backsweep — PASS

The specification appropriately authorizes a retrospective audit of prior:

- `INCONCLUSIVE`;
- `NOT ESTABLISHED`;
- `SPECIFICATION ONLY`;
- `BLOCKED`

findings for constructive follow-up paths.

The original verdicts remain unchanged.

The audit asks whether a finite, explicit resolution path exists; it does not rewrite historical epistemic classifications into the new `INCONCLUSIVE` subclasses.

## 11. Distinct epistemic vocabularies — PASS

The specification correctly avoids flattening readiness classifications and evidentiary verdicts.

For example:

`SPECIFICATION ONLY`

remains a readiness classification.

It is not automatically converted into an `INCONCLUSIVE` subclass.

The constructive-gap framework is an additional follow-up annotation, not a replacement vocabulary for every repository status.

## 12. PI ruling transcription — PASS

The specification correctly transcribes the PI ruling that decisions take effect when issued and that reviews are mandatory but non-gating.

The wording preserves the authority boundary:

review may identify defects, recommend revision, or recommend supersession, but does not suspend a PI decision unless the PI explicitly so rules.

## 13. `CONVENTIONS.md` protection finding — PASS

It does not infer append-only status and does not prematurely adjudicate whether reviewed definitions require supersession rather than in-place modification.

The question remains explicitly open.

The current taxonomy remains:

`CONVENTIONS.md -> definitions / definitional conventions`

`decisions/ -> PI decisions`

## 16. Wording maintenance — PASS

The identified wording corrections are appropriately limited to non-substantive maintenance.

They must not alter scientific meaning, reopen settled findings, or broaden task authority.

Exact before/after text should be recorded.

## 17. No false-positive weakening — PASS

The governance amendment does not lower the standard required for:

- `FOUND`;
- `SUPPORTED`;
- `VERIFIED`;

A positive verdict still requires the evidence demanded by the underlying task.

The amendment changes only what happens after a correct inconclusive result.

## 18. False-negative protection — PASS

The specification adds a useful complementary safeguard:

do not abandon an inconclusive finding when a finite, explicit, separately testable bridge to resolution has been identified.

This improves the programme's ability to recover constructive results without allowing the verifier to complete the missing derivation itself.
