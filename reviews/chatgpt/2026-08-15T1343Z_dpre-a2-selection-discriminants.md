## Scientific review

The revised specification correctly separates two epistemically different operations.

For the H(4)-isotropy question, the task compares alternative interpretations of an already-frozen ontology statement. The resulting discrimination, if any, is therefore **interpretive**: it arises from how an existing commitment is read.

For the finite-range question, the specification no longer disguises a new physical commitment as an interpretation. Case A explicitly adds finite-range microscopic coupling as a new ontology requirement; Case B retains the present ontology without adding that requirement. Any candidate eliminated only under Case A is therefore eliminated **additively**, by a newly adopted commitment rather than by an already-existing one.

The resulting verdict taxonomy — `DISCRIMINATING — INTERPRETIVE`, `DISCRIMINATING — ADDITIVE`, `NOT DISCRIMINATING`, or `NOT ESTABLISHED` as defined by the specification — is scientifically appropriate. Interpretive and additive discrimination must remain separately reported even if both occur.

## Candidate symmetry and bias control

The two-by-four comparison required for the isotropy question is appropriate. All four candidate formulations must be evaluated under both readings rather than examining only the candidate most likely to be affected.

The specification also correctly requires consideration of downstream consequences for the programme's Lorentz-emergence reasoning. An interpretation that excludes a candidate but simultaneously weakens a frozen emergence argument is not a cost-free discriminator and must be reported as such.

Per-candidate treatment-depth checks and the prohibition on ranking or recommendation are suitable safeguards for this task. They do not prove the absence of bias, but they make asymmetric scrutiny visible.

## D-pre-A dossier clarification

The specification correctly preserves the distinction between a lattice species/taste/branch index and the `U(N)` flavour index.

The prior concern that the D-pre-A dossier asserted an outright Wilson/overlap conflict with the common-per-flavour mass ansatz was based on an over-reading. The relevant dossier statement is conditional on treating lattice species as additional flavours. The dossier itself leaves the species-to-flavour/`N` mapping `NOT ESTABLISHED`.

Accordingly, this task must not manufacture a Wilson or overlap incompatibility from an unestablished mapping, and no repair of the D-pre-A dossier is required on that basis.

## Scope assessment

The task boundary is appropriate. It tests two comparatively inexpensive potential discriminants without beginning the much larger transfer-matrix, reflection-positivity, or Euclidean-to-spectral construction.

If neither question discriminates among candidates, `NOT DISCRIMINATING` is a substantive result: it establishes that these ontology-level tests do not justify a canonical kinetic-operator choice and that a more expensive physical discriminator will be required.

If finite range discriminates only after Case A is adopted, the report must state plainly that the discrimination was produced by a new ontology commitment. It must not be represented as something already implied by the current substrate ontology.

## Non-blocking wording note

Where the acceptance criteria refer informally to "one of the three" outcomes, execution should follow the explicit verdict definitions in the specification. The operative distinction is between the top-level non-discriminating/not-established outcomes and the two separately labelled forms of discrimination. No specification amendment is required.
