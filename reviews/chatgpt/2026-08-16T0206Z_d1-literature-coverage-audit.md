## Findings

### 1. Specification completeness — PASS

The uploaded artifact is the complete specification. It begins at §0, contains the network precondition, literature-source reconstruction, applicability rules, verdict taxonomy, search scope, Rule 16 assessment, acceptance criteria, and the verification note.

The earlier concern that the visible material might have begun mid-document does not apply to this uploaded artifact.

### 2. Literature scope — PASS

The specification correctly identifies the B0 source as **three structured literature claims plus the L3 staggered-literature gap**.

Those entries are search seeds rather than a hard search boundary. Directly relevant literature outside B0 must be pursued where it could settle or materially reduce proposition `(ii)` for a named candidate.

### 3. Evidence and fetch discipline — PASS

A work used to support an applicability or coverage conclusion must carry a resolvable identifier and be fetched to the evidential depth required by the conclusion.

Unfetched or recalled material may be recorded, but cannot support `COVERED`. Abstract-only evidence cannot support a load-bearing `COVERED` verdict unless the abstract itself states every hypothesis required for the mapping.

### 4. Applicability standard — PASS

For a relevant theorem–candidate pair, `COVERED` requires explicit mapping of every common axis and every theorem-specific hypothesis.

The specification correctly distinguishes a genuine relevant mismatch (`PARTIAL`) from a theorem outside the candidate's formulation class (`NOT AN APPLICABILITY CANDIDATE`).

Unfrozen programme parameters cannot be silently chosen to make a theorem apply.

### 5. Theorem composition — PASS

A coverage basis may consist of one fetched theorem or a finite set of fetched theorems whose conclusions and hypotheses compose without a new scientific lemma supplied by the executor.

Every composition junction must be explicitly mapped. If a missing junction requires a new proof or lemma, the candidate is not `COVERED` on that basis.

### 6. Transfer matrix versus OS reflection positivity — PASS

The specification preserves the B0 distinction between proposition `(i)` and proposition `(ii)`.

Transfer-matrix-only results are `ROUTE EVIDENCE`; they do not contribute positively to proposition `(ii)` unless the fetched work explicitly establishes OS/reflection positivity or supplies a mapped theorem that logically implies it.

### 7. Verdict taxonomy and burden accounting — PASS

The four candidate verdicts and the discrete burden accounting are internally consistent.

`COVERED` replaces one from-scratch candidate-specific proposition `(ii)` construction unit with theorem-applicability documentation. `PARTIAL` removes zero units. No fractional construction reductions are permitted.

### 8. Candidate neutrality — PASS

The task does not select, rank, or prefer a canonical operator. It does not add a finite-range ontology requirement, decide temporal boundary conditions, treat overlap non-ultralocality as a refutation, or elevate reflection positivity into the only possible discriminator.

### 9. Rule 16 treatment — PASS

The specification correctly states that a fetched theorem or explicitly composable theorem set applies mathematically only after every common axis and theorem-specific hypothesis is mapped.

Literature applicability is not an independent repository proof of the published theorem. Coverage is also not physical evidence favouring a candidate, and `NO COVERAGE FOUND` is bounded by the fetched evidence set.

### 10. Acceptance criteria — PASS

A1–A11 operationalise the scientific requirements with appropriate STOP conditions. In particular:

- A1–A3 bind execution to the authoritative main and network precondition;
- A4 reconstructs the three claims plus L3 gap from `main`;
- A5 records identifier and fetch depth;
- A6 requires full mapping per relevant theorem–candidate pair and separates `ROUTE EVIDENCE`;
- A7 requires four verdicts and discrete burden accounting;
- A8 records encountered but unpursued works;
- A9 prevents selection or proof-route design;
- A10 requires the Rule 16 assessment;
- A11 preserves the no-`main`-touch scope.

No remaining acceptance criterion conflicts with the normative definitions in the body of the specification.
