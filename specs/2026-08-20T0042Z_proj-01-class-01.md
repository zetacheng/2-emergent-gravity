## 1. Why this task exists — a rule conflict, resolved without amending either rule

`P2-RECON-PROJ-01` recorded `Q1` as `INCONCLUSIVE` before rule 22 existed, so
its result carries no subclass and no `Resolution path`.

    rule 22   every INCONCLUSIVE result carries a subclass and a
              Resolution path
    rule 17   an integration MUST NOT add a governance or epistemic
              classification the reviewed results did not carry

An earlier Researcher note proposed adding the subclass at `PROJ-01`'s
integration. **That was wrong: it would have violated rule 17.** The note is
recorded here rather than quietly dropped.

**The resolution weakens neither rule.** This task produces the classification
as its own reviewed result. `PROJ-01`'s integration then carries forward a
classification that *was* reviewed, which is what rule 17 requires.

    PROJ-01 INTEG    transports it — adds nothing

## 3. The classification, determined not asserted

**This specification does not state which subclass applies.** It states the
test, which is rule 22's own, read at `M3`.

The executor determines, from `M1` and `M2` against `M3`:

    Does a candidate positive argument already exist in the evidence,
    and is what is missing a finite, explicit, separately investigable
    set of bridges?

        yes  ->  INCONCLUSIVE — CONSTRUCTIVE GAP IDENTIFIED
        no   ->  INCONCLUSIVE — EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT

**Either outcome is a completed classification.** `CONSTRUCTIVE GAP
IDENTIFIED` is not the expected answer and is not the preferred answer; a
determination of `EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT` is equally a result, and `C4` accepts
both.

If the evidence does not settle the question, the classification is recorded
as `UNDETERMINED BY READING` with the reason, and that too is a completed
measurement.

## 4. The `Resolution path`

Answered as a named field, per rule 22, and **symmetrically**.

For each gap recorded at `M2`, the field states what would resolve it, and
states **both** directions explicitly:

    what would establish the bridge   ... and what that would then permit
    what would refute the bridge      ... and what that would then mean

**A `Resolution path` naming only the confirming outcome is incomplete**, and
`C5` fails on it. This is rule 22's symmetry requirement, and it exists so
that a bounded task opened on a gap is not opened in search of a wanted
answer.

**Two limits are recorded with the path**, because `Q1`'s gaps are not
independent of each other in the way a reader may assume:

1. Establishing any one bridge does **not** by itself change `Q1`'s verdict,
   if another remains open. The path states what each bridge would and would
   not settle **on its own**.
2. A bridge may be refuted. Refutation is a resolution, not a failure of the
   bounded task that pursued it.

## 5. Prohibition on crossing the bridge

The artifact **must not** contain: a derivation of any named bridge, a sketch
or outline of one, an assertion that a bridge holds or fails, or a statement
that a bridge is likely either way.

**Defining what would settle a question is permitted. Settling it is not.**
`C6` verifies this by reading, and passes on absence.

If the executor finds that classifying `Q1` would require performing part of
a bridge, the classification is `UNDETERMINED BY READING` per §3 and the
bridge is not performed.
