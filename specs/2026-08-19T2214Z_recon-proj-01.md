## 3. What counts as a derivational ground — stated before the search

A location is a DERIVATIONAL GROUND only if it satisfies **both**:

    PART 1   it supplies a DERIVATIONAL ARGUMENT — for example from symmetry,
             a Ward or gauge identity, representation theory, dynamics, an
             exact identity, or any other explicit derivation — rather than
             merely defining, asserting, or assuming the projection; AND
    PART 2   it concludes something about the CONTRIBUTION OF THE DISCARDED
             COMPLEMENT to the target observable.

**The examples in Part 1 are illustrative and not a closed list.** The
distinction that matters is *derived* versus *defined or assumed*, not which
mathematical route the derivation takes. A valid derivation that reaches Part
2 by a method not named above still satisfies Part 1; recording it as NOT
FOUND because its method is absent from a pre-registered taxonomy would be a
defect of this test, not a finding about the repository.

**Both parts are required.** A statement that the axis-TT slope *is* the
manuscript's definition of `Z` satisfies neither part: it is a definitional
statement, and `A-EXT-01` already records it as such. A derivation grounding
why some other quantity is universal satisfies Part 1 but not Part 2 unless
it reaches a conclusion about the complement's contribution.

**This test is stated here so that it is fixed before the evidence is read.**
It is not to be relaxed or reinterpreted during the search; a location the
executor believes should count but which fails a part is recorded under `M3`
with the reasoning, not promoted.
