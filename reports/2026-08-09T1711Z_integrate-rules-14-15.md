## 7. Rules 14 and 15, quoted in full as they stand on the integration branch

### 7.1 Rule 14, as landed

    ### 14. Validator outcome contract

    Unless a task specification states a stricter or explicitly different
    requirement, a validator PASSES only when all of the following hold:
    the intended validator process started successfully; it completed
    without timeout or external termination; it returned **exit status
    0**; and no required test, collection phase, or teardown phase was
    skipped or aborted.

    **Output showing `[100%]`, or the absence of an assertion failure,
    does NOT override a non-zero exit status.**

    A task specification may then simply say "validators must pass under
    Rule 14", rather than restating the exit contract each time. Where a
    task genuinely needs different semantics — an expected `exit 5`, a
    deselection, a known `xfail` — the specification states it explicitly.

    **Disposition vocabulary.** Three distinct dispositions:

    - **satisfied** — the criterion genuinely passed as written;
    - **not satisfied, PI-authorized exception** — the criterion was
      evaluated, was not met, and the PI accepts the task nonetheless;
    - **waived** — the PI removed the obligation BEFORE its evaluation
      deadline, so it was never evaluated.

    **A criterion already evaluated and found unsatisfied cannot later be
    reported as waived.** Its disposition is a retrospective exception, or
    task rejection. Waiving after the fact would erase an evaluation that
    actually happened.

    Every waiver and every exception records: the authorizing authority;
    the time or sequence point; the criterion affected; the specific
    evidence accepted; and whether it applies only to this task.
    Record an accepted timeout as:

        A9: NOT SATISFIED
        Disposition: PI-AUTHORIZED EXCEPTION
        Evidence: all selected tests reached completion output with no
                  assertion failure; process exit 124 after 120.2 s.

    **An exception changes the disposition of the TASK, not the historical
    evaluation of the CRITERION.** A criterion that was not satisfied is
    never written back as passed.

    *Incident.* Five validator files each reached `[100%]` with no
    assertion failure and each terminated at ~120.2 s with exit 124.
    Because all five stopped at the same boundary regardless of their
    individual cost, the cost lies in process shutdown rather than in the
    tests. The executor reported this as a qualified condition rather than
    as a pass, which was correct, but the acceptance criterion had no
    vocabulary for the outcome.

### 7.2 Rule 15, as landed

    ### 15. Governing artifacts are committed

    **A specification, a pre-execution review, a task report, and any
    manifest supplied to a checker are governing artifacts and are
    committed to the repository.** An artifact that determines what a task
    was authorised to do, or that records what it did, is not evidence
    while it exists only in a conversation.

    **Placement.** Specifications under `specs/`; task reports under
    `reports/`; pre-execution reviews under `reviews/<function>/`, using
    the existing directory for the reviewing party. Supplied manifests are
    reproduced in the report that used them.

    **Timing.** A specification is committed as the task's first commit.
    **A pre-execution review is committed before the work it authorises
    proceeds.** A report is committed by the task that produced it.

    **Prospective only.** Records created before this rule are not
    retrospectively non-conforming, and are not to be back-filled.

### 15.1 Rule 13 carries two diagnostic orders, and the older one still reads as complete

13's pre-existing sentence, in a later authorised task.

§15.5 raises its practical weight.**

### 15.2 Rule 4 and Rule 15 overlap; Rule 4 asks for more

Rule 4 requires the execution prompt for a decisive or pre-registered run
to be **committed AND its sha256 recorded in the run's report**. Rule 15
requires a specification to be committed as the task's first commit and
says nothing about a digest.

**Not a contradiction — Rule 15 is broader in coverage and weaker in
requirement.** The risk is that a later reader takes Rule 15, as the
newer and more general statement, to be the complete obligation and drops
Rule 4's digest. **Both bind.**

**And a live compliance question I raise without resolving.** Recent
reports — including this one — record the specification's commit SHA and
message but not its sha256. Whether that breaches Rule 4 turns on whether
a governance or integration task is a "decisive or pre-registered run",
which Rule 4 does not define.

### 15.3 Rule 12 and Rule 14 — compatible

Rule 12 requires each acceptance criterion to have "a machine-executable
verification procedure with a defined expected outcome"; Rule 14 adds the
dispositions `satisfied`, `not satisfied, PI-authorized exception`, and
`waived`.

**No conflict.** Rule 12 constrains how a criterion is *written*; Rule 14
how an *outcome* is dispositioned. Rule 14's insistence that an exception
changes the task's disposition and never the criterion's historical
evaluation is exactly what preserves Rule 12's meaning.

## 18. Secondary findings, and what I would have specified differently

**1. `CONVENTIONS.md` still has no structural validator, and it now
carries fifteen rules.** Every prohibition protecting it — do not
renumber, do not reword, do not reorder — is enforced by an executor
choosing to comply and by a reviewer reading a report. **The A5a
extraction in §5 is what actually establishes compliance, and it was
written for this report and does not persist.** A standing test
asserting contiguous numbering from 1, no duplicates, and
`^### <n>. <title>` headings would make the next silent renumbering
impossible to land. **`GATES.md` has `test_gate_anchors.py`;
`CONVENTIONS.md`, the more constitutional file, has nothing.** `tests/`
is protected here, so this is a separate authorization.

**2. A6's count should have been derived, not written.** "All six
earlier 2026-08-08 and 2026-08-09 entries" is a repository-derived
literal, and Rule 12's own refinement requires such literals to be
machine-checked at the evidence base before issue. **A `grep -c` would
have returned 3.** The criterion was still satisfiable because its
substance does not depend on the number, but the number is exactly the
kind of literal the amendment package's Amendment H exists to catch —
and Amendment H is not yet landed.

**3. The two cross-references in §15.5 and §15.6 should be swept, not
found.** Both were discovered by grepping `main` for rule-number
references after the merge. **Nothing required me to look**, and a later
integration adding Rule 16 or 17 has no obligation to. **A criterion of
the form "list every document on `main` that references a rule by number
or by section title, and state whether the reference still resolves
correctly"** would make this systematic. It is cheap — one grep — and it
found two real items on the first attempt.

**4. Rule 15's fourth clause needs an actor before it binds**, and §16
gives the first concrete instance. The rule is integrated unaltered; the
gap is that nobody is named, no executor can perform it, and no check can
notice its absence.

**5. Recurring, and raised for the tenth time.** The `CONVENTIONS.md`
index entry for conventions a computation consumes is still unwritten,
and five decisions still live only as dated `DECISION_LOG.md` entries,
two of them located by exact heading text by a script already on `main`.
**Rule 15 does not close this** — it requires governing artifacts to be
committed, and those entries are committed; what is missing is that they
are not discoverable from `CONVENTIONS.md`. **Landing Rule 15 could
easily be read as having closed it, and it has not.**
