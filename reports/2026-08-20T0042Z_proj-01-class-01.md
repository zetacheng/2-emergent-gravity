### `S-3` — this was the first operational use of rule 22, and one thing about the rule is worth reporting

§12 says the task's purpose "is as much to test whether the mechanism works as
to classify one finding", so the mechanism's behaviour is reportable.

**What worked.** The two-subclass test was decidable by reading, on a real
finding, without entering the physics. The symmetry requirement was not a
formality — it forced the refuting direction to be written for both gaps, and
for `GAP-A` that direction was already in the source and would have been
dropped by a one-directional path.

**What the rule does not say, and what this task had to decide.** Rule 22 says
a subclass is "assigned when the result is recorded". **It does not say who
assigns a subclass to a result recorded before the rule existed**, nor what
label such an assignment carries. §1a of this specification supplied both — a
separate reviewed task, and the `RETROSPECTIVE CLASSIFICATION` label modelled
on ruling 4's treatment of the historical `reviews/pi/` records.

**That is a governance construction this specification made, and rule 22 does
not contain it.** It worked here. **Whether every retrospective classification
must be its own reviewed task, or whether the retrospective audit named at §13.3
may classify in bulk, is not settled by this task and is not proposed here.**
Recorded for the Researcher because the audit will meet it immediately.
